Overview

A road-and-fly trip through interior and Arctic Alaska — Denali, Gates of the Arctic, Wrangell–St. Elias, and Kobuk Valley — followed by a week cruising Hubbard Glacier, Glacier Bay, and the Inside Passage. Five days in the middle are still open for Lake Clark and Katmai.

Detailed Day Plans

June 27 — Talkeetna (Denali Flightseeing)

Ideal — flight goes

  • 8:00am — Breakfast at Flying Squirrel Bakery Café (Sunday hours 8am–4pm)
  • 9:00am — Check in with your flightseeing operator, safety briefing
  • 9:30–11:30am — Denali flightseeing with glacier landing (~1.5–2 hrs; exact slot depends on the operator's schedule that day)
  • 12:00pm — Lunch at West Rib Pub & Grill
  • 1:00–2:00pm — Talkeetna Historical Society Museum (open 9am–5pm daily in summer)
  • 2:00–3:30pm — Walk Main Street, Nagley's Store, the river confluence viewpoint (Susitna/Talkeetna/Chulitna)
  • 3:30–5:00pm — Downtime at Talkeetna Alaskan Lodge's mountain-view deck — afternoon often has the clearest Denali views
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at Denali Brewing Co. (open until 10pm)
  • Evening — Casual stroll; it barely gets dark this close to solstice

Operators: K2 Aviation, Talkeetna Air Taxi, or Sheldon Air Service, all out of Talkeetna Airport (TKA).

Alternate — flight weathered out

  • 8:00am — Breakfast at Flying Squirrel Bakery Café
  • 9:30–11:30am — Mahay's Jet Boat Adventures, Wilderness Jet Boat Tour on the Susitna River (a historic homestead stop en route); confirm exact departure time with the operator when booking
  • 12:00–2:00pm — Talkeetna Sled Dog Kennel Tour — van pickup downtown, musher demo, wheeled-cart ride through the woods with the dogs
  • 2:00–3:00pm — Lunch at Talkeetna Roadhouse (open 8am–8pm daily)
  • 3:00–4:00pm — Talkeetna Historical Society Museum (closes 5pm — moved earlier in the day to fit)
  • 4:00–6:00pm — Self-paced choice: canoe/kayak at Talkeetna Lakes Park, or a scenic drive along the Talkeetna Spur Road pullouts for Alaska Range views if skies are partly clearing. (Denali Zipline Tours is a ~3-hr guided tour — if you'd rather do that, swap it in for the jet boat or kennel slot earlier in the day rather than squeezing it in here; confirm departure times when booking.)
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at Mountain High Pizza Pie (open until 9pm Sundays)
  • Evening — Worth calling your flightseeing operator back — daylight lasts into the late evening this close to solstice, and conditions sometimes clear enough for a late flight; ask to be put on standby

June 28 — Talkeetna to Fairbanks (Denali National Park Green Bus)

Road status note: the Denali Park Road has been closed at Mile 43 (East Fork Bridge) since 2021 due to the Pretty Rocks Landslide. NPS has targeted full bus service to Eielson/Wonder Lake/Kantishna to resume in 2027, but hasn't confirmed a date — the plan below is built around the Mile 43 turnaround as the safe assumption. Worth reconfirming on the NPS Denali "current conditions" page closer to your travel date; if the full road has reopened by then, a longer bus ride becomes possible but likely wouldn't fit in the same day as the drive to Fairbanks.
  • 6:30am — Depart Talkeetna Alaskan Lodge, drive to Denali National Park entrance (~2 hrs)
  • 8:30am — Arrive Denali Bus Depot / Wilderness Access Center, check in for your reserved Transit Bus departure (book ahead at reservedenali.com; arrive 20 min early)
  • 9:00am (approx.) — Board your reserved green Transit Bus toward East Fork Bridge (Mile 43) — exact departure time is assigned when you book tickets, not fixed at 9am — hop-off privileges anywhere beyond Savage River for wildlife viewing and photos, reboard any later green bus; round trip ~4.5 hrs at the current Mile 43 turnaround
  • 1:30pm — Return to Denali Bus Depot
  • 1:30–2:00pm — Quick lunch at the Morino Grill, Denali Visitor Center
  • 2:00–3:00pm — Sled Dog Kennels demonstration (free shuttle from the Denali Visitor Center, 2:00pm show)
  • 3:00pm — Depart the park, continue drive to Fairbanks (~2.5–3 hrs)
  • 5:30–6:00pm — Arrive Fairbanks, check into Westmark Fairbanks
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at Lavelle's Bistro

June 29 — Fairbanks to Coldfoot (Dalton Highway)

Meal-timing note: Coldfoot Camp Cafe runs buffet-only service in summer — breakfast buffet 5–9am, dinner buffet 5–9pm. Between those windows, only truckers can order off the menu, so the plan below eats lunch on the road rather than at Coldfoot.
  • 7:00am — Depart Westmark Fairbanks; top off fuel and grab supplies in Fairbanks — it's the last reliable gas before Coldfoot (~240 miles)
  • 7:00–9:15am — Drive to the Yukon River Bridge (~135 miles)
  • 9:15–9:45am — Stop at Yukon River Camp (open 8:00am–11:30pm daily) — restrooms, coffee/snacks, photos of the Yukon River and the Trans-Alaska Pipeline
  • 9:45–11:00am — Drive to the Arctic Circle sign (Mile 115)
  • 11:00–11:30am — Arctic Circle photo stop
  • 11:30am–1:00pm — Drive to Coldfoot (~60 miles)
  • 1:00pm — Arrive Coldfoot; drop bags at Slate Creek Inn (early check-in isn't guaranteed, but bags can usually be left); check in with Coyote Air at Coldfoot Airport about tomorrow's flight
  • 1:30–4:30pm — Arctic Interagency Visitor Center — exhibits, bookstore, short nature trail to the Trans-Alaska Pipeline viewpoint (bring snacks for the afternoon, since the cafe is buffet-only at meal windows)
  • 5:00–6:00pm — Dinner buffet at Coldfoot Camp Cafe (5–9pm buffet window)
  • Evening — Optional evening program at the Arctic Interagency Visitor Center, if one's scheduled that night

June 30 — Coldfoot (Gates of the Arctic Flightseeing)

Ideal — flight goes

  • 8:00–8:45am — Breakfast buffet at Coldfoot Camp Cafe (5–9am window)
  • 9:00am — Check in at the Coyote Air office, Coldfoot Airport — no fixed departure time; they'll call you when conditions and the day's flight queue allow
  • Late morning/midday — Gates of the Arctic Tour flight to Frigid Crags and Boreal Mountain via Holmes Pass (~2 hrs total, weather permitting)
  • Afternoon — Free time: the nature trail to the Trans-Alaska Pipeline viewpoint at the Arctic Interagency Visitor Center, or just relax — Coldfoot itself is tiny
  • 5:00–6:00pm — Dinner buffet at Coldfoot Camp Cafe
  • Evening — Optional evening program at the Arctic Interagency Visitor Center, if offered

Alternate — flight weathered out

  • 8:00–8:45am — Breakfast buffet at Coldfoot Camp Cafe
  • 9:00am — Check in with Coyote Air — expect to be asked to check back later rather than a firm no, so keep the day loose
  • 9:30–10:00am — Drive north to Wiseman (~13 miles, ~30 min) — historic 1907 gold-rush village, home to about a dozen year-round residents; Wiseman Historical Museum (mining photos and equipment) and a handful of original cabins
  • 10:00–11:15am — Continue north to the Sukakpak Mountain viewpoint (Mile 204) — a striking marble peak thrust up during the Brooks Range's formation, good photo stop
  • 11:15am–12:15pm — Drive back to Coldfoot
  • 12:15–1:00pm — Packed lunch/snacks (the cafe is buffet-only at meal windows — no walk-in lunch service between 9am and 5pm)
  • 1:00–4:00pm — Arctic Interagency Visitor Center exhibits and trails; check back with Coyote Air periodically in case conditions improve
  • 5:00–6:00pm — Dinner buffet at Coldfoot Camp Cafe
  • Evening — Daylight lasts well past 10pm this close to solstice — worth one more check with Coyote Air before writing the day off; if it's a firm no, July 1 morning is the last shot before driving south

July 1 — Coldfoot to Fairbanks

If June 30's flight went (relaxed drive-back day)

  • 8:00–8:45am — Breakfast buffet at Coldfoot Camp Cafe
  • 9:00am — Depart Coldfoot, drive to Fairbanks (~5.5–6 hrs)
  • 2:30–3:00pm — Arrive Fairbanks, check into Westmark Fairbanks
  • 3:30–6:00pm — University of Alaska Museum of the North (open 9am–7pm, admission until 6:30pm)
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at The Pump House Restaurant (opens 4pm)

If June 30's flight was weathered out (last-chance backup day)

  • 7:00am — Check with Coyote Air first thing — if conditions look workable, plan to fly this morning
  • 7:30–9:30am (if flying) — Gates of the Arctic Tour flight, weather permitting
  • 10:00am — Depart Coldfoot regardless of outcome, drive to Fairbanks (~5.5–6 hrs)
  • ~3:30–4:00pm (if flew) or ~1:00pm (if grounded) — Arrive Fairbanks, check into Westmark Fairbanks
  • Remaining afternoon — Museum of the North if there's time before the 6:30pm admission cutoff; otherwise just rest
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at The Pump House Restaurant

If Gates of the Arctic still doesn't happen after both attempts, there's no ground alternative to fall back on — unlike Denali or Wrangell–St. Elias, this park is fly-in only, so missing it means missing the park entirely.

Buffer risk worth flagging: as currently built, Gates of the Arctic gets exactly two attempts — June 30 and the July 1 morning backup — before you're committed to driving south. Two consecutive bad-weather days at Coldfoot would mean missing the park outright, with no later point in the trip to circle back. Worth deciding whether to add a third buffer day at Coldfoot (pulled from the Lake Clark/Katmai reserve, currently at 4 days).

July 2 — Fairbanks to Copper Center

  • 9:00am — Depart Westmark Fairbanks, final fuel stop in Fairbanks
  • 9:00am–2:30pm — Drive Fairbanks → Copper Center via Richardson Hwy (~5.5 hrs, ~250 miles)
  • 2:30pm — Arrive Copper Center, check into Copper River Princess Wilderness Lodge
  • 3:00–4:00pm — Wrangell–St. Elias National Park Visitor Center (open 9am–5pm daily) — exhibits, relief model of the park, ranger talk if one's scheduled
  • 4:15–5:00pm — George I. Ashby Memorial Museum (hours found were inconsistent across sources — worth a quick call ahead; historic log cabins with early mining and Russian artifacts)
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at Two Rivers Restaurant (or the more casual Whistle Stop Bar & Grill), both at the lodge

Optional, book ahead: Mount Wrangell flightseeing with Copper Valley Air Service4 (Gulkana Airport, Glennallen, ~20–25 min away) — no walk-in hours; "Three Peak Tour" (~60 min, ~$400/person, Mt. Sanford/Mt. Drum/Mt. Wrangell) or "Flight of Majesty" (~120 min, adds mud volcanoes and wildlife) — would replace one of the stops above given the day's long drive.

July 3 — Copper Center to Kennecott/McCarthy

  • 8:00am — Breakfast at Copper River Princess Wilderness Lodge (buffet, 6–10am)
  • 9:00am — Depart Copper Center, drive to the McCarthy Road footbridge trailhead (~4–5 hrs, incl. ~60 miles of gravel)
  • 1:00–2:00pm — Arrive footbridge, park, cross on foot into McCarthy
  • 2:00pm — Catch the Copper Town Shuttle or Blackburn Heritage Shuttle (both run hourly from the footbridge/McCarthy) up to Kennecott (~20 min ride)
  • 2:30pm — Arrive Kennecott, check into Kennicott Glacier Lodge
  • 3:00–5:00pm — Self-guided walk through the Kennecott townsite — most historic buildings are visible from outside without the paid tour
  • 6:00pm — Dinner at Kennicott Glacier Lodge

July 4 — Kennecott (Root Glacier + Mill Tour)

  • 8:00am — Breakfast at Kennicott Glacier Lodge
  • 9:00am–1:30pm — Root Glacier guided half-day hike (St. Elias Alpine Guides — crampons provided, ~4.5 hrs)
  • 1:30–2:30pm — Lunch (packed lunch or quick bite at the lodge)
  • 3:00–5:00pm — Kennecott Mill Town Tour (St. Elias Alpine Guides, fixed 2-hr guided tour — the only way inside the 14-story concentration mill; check in 10 min early at their office, second building on the left entering town)
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at Kennicott Glacier Lodge, or walk into McCarthy for a change of scenery at McCarthy Lodge
This fits both Kennecott essentials into 2 days and satisfies the lodge's 2-night minimum. It does mean skipping the dedicated Wrangell–St. Elias flightseeing day10 — worth reconsidering during the buffer pass, either as an early-morning add-on before the Root Glacier hike on July 4, or reinstated as a third day if you'd rather not compress further.

July 5 — Kennecott to Anchorage (Matanuska Glacier Helicopter Tour)

  • 7:00am — Breakfast at Kennicott Glacier Lodge, check out
  • 7:30am — Shuttle to the footbridge, cross, retrieve the car
  • 8:00am–12:30pm — Drive McCarthy Road → Richardson Hwy → Glenn Hwy to Sheep Mountain Lodge (~4.5 hrs)
  • 12:30–1:00pm — Lunch at Sheep Mountain Lodge
  • 1:00–2:00pm — Matanuska Glacier helicopter tour with Sheep Mountain Air — Blue Pool Glacier Landing Tour (45 min: 20 min in air, 25 min on the ice, ~$299/person) is a solid default; the 2-Landing Tour (90 min, ~$399/person) is there if you'd rather make this the day's centerpiece
  • 2:00–4:30pm — Continue drive to Anchorage (~2–2.5 hrs)
  • 4:30–5:00pm — Arrive Anchorage, check into Hotel Captain Cook
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at Glacier BrewHouse

July 6 — Anchorage to Kotzebue

Taking the early flight turns this from a pure travel day into a bonus buffer day for Kobuk Valley — you land with the afternoon still open instead of arriving in the evening.
  • 7:00am — Depart Hotel Captain Cook, head to Ted Stevens Anchorage International (ANC)
  • 8:40–9:00am — Flight ANC → OTZ (search flights, Alaska Airlines, ~1 hr 40 min)
  • ~10:30am — Arrive Kotzebue
  • 11:00am — Check into Nullaqvik Hotel, drop bags
  • 11:30am — Check in with Golden Eagle Outfitters at their hangar near the Alaska terminal — let them know you're in town; since they fly in queue order as conditions allow, there's a real chance of getting the Kobuk Valley flight in this same afternoon
  • Early–mid afternoon — Kobuk Valley flightseeing if a slot opens up (weather permitting); if not, this becomes free time instead
  • 1:00–3:00pm (if no flight) — Northwest Arctic Heritage Center, walk Shore Avenue along the lagoon
  • 6:30pm — Dinner at Bayside Restaurant
